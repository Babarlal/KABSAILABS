AI receptionist cost in 2026: what seven providers charge, priced on the same month of calls

By Babar Lal  ·  15 October 2026  ·  10 min read

An HVAC owner opens four pricing pages. One says $49 a month. One says $79. One says $150. One says $720. They look easy to compare until you read the small print.

The $49 plan counts minutes. The $79 plan counts callers. The $150 plan counts calls. The $720 plan is human receptionists, also counted in minutes. None of the four tells you what your month will cost.

So I took the published prices from seven providers in October 2026 and priced the same month of calls on each one.

The short answer

For a business taking about 150 calls a month, AI receptionist plans came to about $119 to $338 a month in October 2026. A human answering service for the same calls costs several times more. The plan that looks cheapest is often not, because each provider counts something different.

The four ways AI receptionists charge

Per minute. You buy a bundle of minutes. Rosie, My AI Front Desk and Ruby work this way. Long calls eat the bundle fast.

Per call. You buy a bundle of calls, whatever their length. Smith.ai works this way. Long calls cost the same as short ones.

Per caller. You pay for each different person who calls in a month, however often they ring. Goodcall works this way, and says it has no per-minute charges.

Build your own. You pay a voice platform such as Retell by the minute, with no bundle. It is the cheapest to run and the most work to set up.

The costs that are not on the pricing page

Overage. Rosie and Ruby do not show what an extra minute costs on their pricing pages. Ask before you sign, because a busy month is when you need the cover most.

Features on the higher plan. Booking into your calendar, warm transfers and CRM links often sit one plan up. Price the plan that does the job, not the cheapest one.

Live backup. Smith.ai lists a human add-on per call but not its price. If you want a person on the hard calls, get that number in writing.

Setup and trials. One accountant on AccountingWEB in February 2026 was quoted £200 for a one-month trial. Another member asked why a trial should cost anything at all.

Yearly billing. My AI Front Desk drops from $99 to $79 a month if you pay yearly. Rosie gives two months free. Both lock you in. Run it monthly until you know it works.

Your time. Someone has to write the call script, connect the calendar, test it with real calls and fix it when it gets something wrong. On a build, add hosting, monitoring and extra capacity. Retell, for example, charges $8 a month per extra concurrent call beyond 20.

What it costs to build your own

Retell's pricing page shows the parts. In October 2026, its voice platform was $0.055 a minute. A standard voice was $0.015. Calls over its Twilio connection were $0.015. The AI model on top ranged from $0.0016 a minute for GPT 5 nano to $0.08 for Claude 4.5 Sonnet. GPT 4.1 was $0.045.

With GPT 4.1, that adds up to about $0.13 a minute. A phone number is $2 a month. New accounts get $10 of free credit.

The meter is cheap. The work is not. You need the call script, the calendar and CRM connections, the rules for when to hand off to a person, and someone watching the call logs each week. When it is not worth it

A few calls a week. If you can answer them yourself, or call back within the hour, a plan costs more than the calls it saves. A missed call text back may be enough.

Calls that are really sales meetings. If most callers want a detailed quote from you, an agent can take details and book a time, but it cannot close the job.

Nowhere to book into. If you have no shared calendar or job system, the agent can only take messages. Fix that first. Our post on automatic appointment booking covers it.

Not timing your calls. Picking a per-minute plan for long intake calls, or a per-call plan for lots of ten-second "are you open?" calls, wastes money every month.

Questions people ask

How much does an AI receptionist cost per month? Published plans started at $29 to $79 a month in October 2026. On our example month of 150 calls at 3 minutes each, AI plans came to about $119 to $338. Human answering services came to about $1,440 to $1,725 for the same calls.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human answering service? Usually, per call. In October 2026, Smith.ai's human plans charged $8.50 to $11.50 for each call over the plan, while AI plans worked out at well under $3 a call. A human still handles angry callers, emergencies and unusual requests better, so many businesses use AI first and a person as backup.

Is it better to pay per minute or per call? It depends on how long your calls run. Short calls, like booking and opening hours, are cheap per minute. Long calls, like quotes and intake, are cheaper per call or per caller. Time ten of your real calls before you choose a plan.

What hidden costs should I ask about? Ask for the overage rate, which features sit on the higher plans, what a transfer to a live person costs, whether there is a setup or paid trial fee, and what the annual price locks you into. Also ask who sets up the calendar and CRM connections, and who fixes the script when it gets something wrong.

How much does it cost to build your own AI receptionist? The running cost is low. Retell's pricing page listed about $0.13 a minute in October 2026 for a typical setup with GPT 4.1 and its telephony, plus $2 a month per phone number. The real cost is building, testing and maintaining it, which is why most small businesses buy a plan or pay someone to build it.

When is an AI receptionist not worth the money? When you get only a few calls a week and can answer them yourself, when most calls are complex sales conversations that need you, or when you have no calendar or job system for it to book into. A missed call text back may be enough at that stage.
