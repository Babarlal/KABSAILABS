Where is my order: how to stop answering it by hand, with tracking updates and automated answers

By Babar Lal  ·  6 October 2026  ·  9 min read

On Friday evening, a customer orders an $85 skincare set from a Shopify store. The store packs it on Monday. Nobody adds the tracking number until Tuesday afternoon, so no shipping email goes out. On Tuesday morning she emails to ask where it is. That evening she emails again. On Wednesday she opens the chat and gets "We reply within 48 hours."

On Wednesday afternoon she calls her bank and files a chargeback for an item not received. The parcel arrives on Thursday. The store now has a dispute on an order it delivered.

The short answer

Tell customers where their order is before they ask. Then let a chat answer from the real order for anyone who still does.

How big the problem is

Most of the data here comes from vendors who sell helpdesks or tracking tools. I label each one.

Gorgias, a helpdesk vendor, says WISMO is the most common question in ecommerce. It puts the share at 18% of incoming requests on average, from its own customer data. The article is from 2023 and does not give a sample size.

Narvar, a post-purchase vendor, surveyed 3,461 US online shoppers in August 2025 for its State of Post-Purchase report. Two-thirds said they feel anxious after clicking buy. 74% had experienced a late delivery. 73% said delivery estimates affect whether they buy, and 40% said they will not buy without one. When something goes wrong, shoppers wanted acknowledgement, a clear explanation and real-time updates without having to ask.

In a 2026 Shopify Community thread, one contributor estimated that around 40% of support queries are order status. That is a guess from one person, not a study. Your own share could be higher or lower, so count it.

Why WISMO tickets happen

No shipping confirmation. The email never went out, or it went to spam. A merchant on the Shopify Community wrote: "I keep getting emails from customers asking me if their orders have shipped. I have enabled shipping and tracking emails and notifications."

Tracking that does not update. A label is printed, but the carrier has not scanned the parcel yet. The customer sees no movement for days.

Carrier delays. The parcel is stuck in a hub, and nobody tells the customer.

Split shipments. Two items arrive, one is missing, and the customer thinks it is lost.

Marketplace orders the store cannot see. An order on Amazon or TikTok Shop lives in another system. It is the same split-systems problem I described in our post on stopping overselling across Shopify, Amazon and TikTok Shop.

Unclear delivery estimates. "Ships in 3 to 5 days" gets read as "arrives in 3 to 5 days".

How to fix it, step by step

  1. Show a clear delivery estimate at checkout and on the product page. Give a date range, not "fast shipping".
  2. Use a branded order status page. Shopify's order status page lets customers check their shipment without contacting you. A tracking app can add your branding and clearer carrier statuses.
  3. Send proactive updates: shipped, delayed, out for delivery and delivered. The delay alert matters most, because it answers the question before the customer has to ask.
  4. Answer anyone who still asks with a chat or AI that looks up the real order. It asks for the order number and email, then reads the order and the carrier's tracking. Our posts on AI website chat and WhatsApp and SMS customer support cover how those conversations work.
  5. Hand lost, damaged and "delivered but not here" cases to a person, with the order details already attached.

The AI should answer from your order data, plus your shipping and returns policies. Our knowledge base service is how we load those policies so it quotes them instead of guessing.

The rules

This is a plain summary, not legal advice. Check your situation with a lawyer.

The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule covers shipping times. You need a reasonable basis for any shipping time you state. If you state no time, you must ship within 30 days. If you cannot ship on time, you must tell the customer. The notice needs a revised date, or a statement that you do not know one, and a way to cancel at your expense. If you cannot get their consent to the delay, you must cancel and refund promptly. So an honest delay alert is not just good service.

Shipping updates by text need the customer's agreement, usually a checkbox at checkout. Honor STOP replies straight away. I covered carrier registration and STOP rules in our post on missed call text back and the US texting rules.

When it backfires

Tracking pages that show wrong data. A page that says "delivered" when the parcel is still in a van causes more tickets, not fewer. Test it with real orders from each carrier you use.

Too many notifications. Eight emails for one parcel trains people to ignore them. Keep to the ones that change what the customer does: shipped, delayed, out for delivery, delivered.

An AI that promises dates. If it tells a customer "Thursday" and the carrier says Monday, you made a promise you cannot keep. Quote the carrier's date and say it is the carrier's.

No route to a person. A customer with a stolen parcel who only gets a tracking link will go to the bank.

Questions people ask

What does WISMO mean? WISMO stands for "where is my order". It is the name online stores use for messages asking about order status, shipping or delivery. For many stores it is the most common type of support message.

How do I reduce WISMO tickets on Shopify? Show a clear delivery estimate before checkout. Make sure shipping emails are switched on and reaching inboxes. Send a delay alert when a parcel slips. Then let a chat or AI look up the real order for anyone who still asks, and hand lost or damaged parcels to a person.

Does Shopify send tracking updates automatically? Shopify sends shipping confirmation, shipping update, out for delivery and delivered notifications by default. The last two depend on tracking events from the carrier or fulfillment app, so they vary by carrier. Shopify does not send a delay alert on its own.

What does the FTC require if an order ships late? Under the FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, you need a reasonable basis for any shipping time you state. If you cannot ship on time, you must tell the customer, give a revised date if you can, and offer the option to cancel for a refund. This is not legal advice.

Can an AI chatbot answer "where is my order"? Yes, if it can look up the real order and the carrier's tracking. It should ask for the order number and email, quote only the carrier's dates, and hand anything lost, damaged or disputed to a person.

What should I do when tracking says delivered but the customer says it never arrived? Hand it to a person straight away. Check the address on the order, the carrier's delivery scan and any photo, and ask the customer to check with neighbors. Then follow your written policy on carrier claims, refunds or reships, and reply before they go to their bank.
