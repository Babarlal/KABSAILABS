How to reduce no-shows: reminders, one-tap rescheduling and deposits that work

By Babar Lal  ·  5 October 2026  ·  9 min read

It is 2pm on a Thursday at a dental office. The hygienist is ready. The room is set up. Nobody comes. At 2:15 the front desk calls the patient. "Oh no, I completely forgot." She rebooks for next month. But the 2pm hour is gone, and nobody can sell it again.

That empty hour is the real cost of a no-show. Most of the fixes are cheap, and many are already in the tools you pay for.

How big the problem is

Most of the good research comes from healthcare. I give each study's age.

The best summary is a 2018 review in the journal Health Policy by Dantas and colleagues. It is eight years old. It looked at 105 studies and found an average no-show rate of about 23%. Studies from North America averaged 23.5%. The strongest links to no-shows were a long wait between booking and the appointment, and a history of past no-shows.

On reminders, the key source is a Cochrane review of text message reminders from 2013. That makes it 13 years old. It pooled eight trials with 6,615 people. Attendance was 67.8% with no reminder and 78.6% with text reminders. Text reminders worked about as well as phone call reminders. In two studies, they cost less per attended visit. The authors rated the evidence low to moderate quality.

Reminders are not a cure. In an MGMA poll from August 2026, 32% of medical groups said their no-show rate was higher this year than in 2025. MGMA is a trade body for practice managers, and 190 groups answered. Some said they did not know why their rate went up, even after adding more reminders.

I could not find solid, openable research on no-shows for salons or trades.

Why people miss appointments

They forget. This is the one reminders fix.

They booked too far ahead. A slot booked six weeks out is easy to lose track of. The 2018 review found long lead times were one of the strongest predictors.

They cannot easily cancel or reschedule. If the only way to change a booking is to call during office hours, many people just do not come.

Cost or transport gets in the way. Practices in the MGMA poll blamed higher patient costs, fuel and work conflicts.

The slot no longer suits them. Nobody offered them another time.

What actually works, step by step

  1. Send a confirmation at booking. It gives the date, time, address and a link to reschedule.
  2. Send a reminder one to two days before. Ask the person to confirm or reschedule.
  3. Send a short reminder on the day, a few hours before.
  4. Make confirming or rescheduling one tap. A cancelled slot should open on your calendar at once. Our post on automatic appointment booking covers how to offer real open times.
  5. Keep a waitlist. When a slot frees up, text the waitlist and give it to the first person who taps.
  6. Take a deposit or card on file for long or high-value slots, like a new patient exam or a color appointment.

Some people will reply with a question instead of tapping. Someone has to answer within minutes, for the reasons in our post on speed to lead. If they call instead, an AI receptionist that answers and reschedules can handle it.

The rules

This is a plain summary, not legal advice. Check your setup with your provider or a lawyer.

Texting first. The CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices treat reminders as informational texts, not promotional ones. You still need the customer's agreement to get texts at that number. Ask for it at booking. The FCC rule at 47 CFR 64.1200 treats replies like stop, quit, end, cancel and unsubscribe as a request to stop. Your system must honor them. Your number also needs carrier registration, which I covered in our post on missed call text back and the US texting rules.

Clinics have HIPAA to think about too. HHS says in its FAQ on appointment reminders that reminders are part of treatment and can be sent without a separate authorization. Its FAQ on leaving messages says to limit the information you disclose. The HHS guidance on email says to use reasonable safeguards and to offer another method if a patient asks. In practice, keep the text to the practice name, date, time and how to reschedule. Leave out the diagnosis, the treatment and anything that hints at either.

Deposits and fees need a clear policy. State it at booking, in writing, before you take a card. For physicians, the AMA's Code of Medical Ethics Opinion 11.3.2 ties missed-appointment fees to the practice's published policy. Also check your insurance contracts and state rules before charging patients.

When reminders backfire

Too many messages. Three texts for a 15-minute visit feels like nagging. People start ignoring them.

Reminders sent to cancelled bookings. This happens when the reminder tool reads a different calendar from the one your staff edit. The customer turns up for a slot that no longer exists.

No easy way to reschedule. "See you tomorrow at 2pm" helps someone who forgot. It does nothing for someone who cannot come. Every reminder needs a reschedule link or a reply that someone answers.

Fees that drive customers away. Charging a loyal customer who had an emergency costs more than the slot. Use deposits for the slots that matter, and use judgement.

Questions people ask

What is a normal no-show rate? It depends on the business. A 2018 review of 105 healthcare studies put the average at about 23%, and 23.5% for North America. Many practices are lower than that. The number that matters is yours, so count last month's no-shows and divide by the appointments you booked.

Do appointment reminder texts reduce no-shows? Yes, in the research we have. A 2013 Cochrane review of healthcare trials found attendance of 78.6% with text reminders against 67.8% with no reminder. Text reminders worked about as well as phone call reminders and cost less. The evidence was rated low to moderate quality.

When should I send appointment reminders? A common pattern is a confirmation at booking, a reminder one to two days before, and a short reminder on the day. I have not found strong research on the single best timing. Test it on your own customers and watch your no-show rate.

Can a medical or dental office text appointment reminders under HIPAA? HHS says appointment reminders are part of treatment and can be sent without a separate authorization. It also says to limit what the message reveals. Keep reminders to the practice name, date, time and how to reschedule, with no diagnosis or treatment details. This is not legal advice.

Should I charge a no-show fee? Only if you state the policy clearly at booking and apply it fairly. A deposit or card on file works best for long or high-value slots. Waive the first miss for a good customer. Test that your tool can actually collect the fee before you rely on it.

How much do automated appointment reminders cost? Often nothing extra.
