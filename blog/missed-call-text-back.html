Missed call text back: the cheapest way to stop losing callers to the next business on Google

By Babar Lal  ·  5 October 2026  ·  9 min read

A man's car is making a grinding noise. He searches "brake repair near me" and taps the first shop. It rings four times and goes to voicemail. He does not leave a message. He goes back to the search results and taps the second shop. They answer. By the time the first shop sees the missed call, the car is already on someone else's lift.

Nobody did anything wrong. The owner was busy doing the work. Missed call text back is the cheapest fix I know for this, though not the most complete one. Here is what it does, what it costs, the US rules, and when you need something bigger.

The short answer

Missed call text back texts anyone whose call you did not answer, within seconds. It is not a receptionist. It only works if the caller is willing to text.

Why callers do not leave voicemails, and why text works better

Voicemail is where calls go to die. Invoca's 2024 analysis of home services calls found that fewer than 3% of callers who reach voicemail leave a message. The same analysis found 27% of calls to those businesses went unanswered.

Clinics see the same. Patient Prism analysed 11.5 million dental calls for its July 2026 report. Of every 100 callers, 31 hung up before reaching anyone.

A text gets a different reaction. SimpleTexting surveyed 1,000 US consumers in January 2026 for its texting statistics report. It found 64% want to be able to text a business back. More people said they prefer to text a business with a question (33%) than call (18%). And 74% said they check text notifications within five minutes. SimpleTexting sells texting software, so treat it as a vendor survey. The reason is simple. A voicemail asks the caller to wait. A text arrives while they are still holding their phone.

How it works, step by step

  1. A call comes in and nobody answers within your ring timeout.
  2. Your phone system texts the caller straight away, names the business, and asks what they need.
  3. The caller replies. Now you have a two-way text conversation instead of a lost lead.
  4. The next reply comes from a booking link, a simple automation, or an AI that offers open slots.
  5. When the job is real, the conversation lands with you or your office, with the details already written down.

Two settings matter. Send one text per caller in a set window, not one per call. And make sure someone sees the replies within minutes. A text back nobody answers is just a slower voicemail.

The US rules that matter

Skipping these rules gets your texts blocked. This is a plain summary, not legal advice. Check your situation with your provider or a lawyer.

First, registration. Business texts sent from ordinary local numbers through software are called A2P 10DLC traffic. The Campaign Registry runs the system where brands and campaigns are verified before messages go out. You do not register directly. Your texting provider does it for you. HighLevel's setup guide says local numbers need A2P 10DLC registration before they can send these texts. Quo's pricing page lists a one-time $19.50 review fee and $1.50 to $3 a month for it.

Second, consent. The carrier industry's CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices separate conversational, informational and promotional texts. Promotional texts need express written permission. A single reply about a call someone just made sits at the safe end. Never add a discount or review request to it.

Third, opt-out. The FCC's rule at 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10) treats replies like stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel and unsubscribe as a valid way to withdraw consent. Requests must be honored within a reasonable time, and no later than ten business days. You may send one confirmation text, as long as it only confirms the opt-out. Set your system to stop the moment it sees one.

When it is not enough

Text back fails in emergencies, because someone with a burst pipe or a broken tooth wants a person now. It fails with callers who will not text. And it fails at volume, when your replies become the new bottleneck.

Questions people ask

What is missed call text back? It is a feature that sends a text to anyone whose call you did not answer, usually within a few seconds. The text says sorry you missed them and asks how you can help. The caller replies by text, and you or an automation carry on the conversation from there.

Do I need A2P 10DLC registration to send missed call texts? If you send them from a normal US local number through a software platform, yes in practice. Business texts on local numbers go through 10DLC, and your provider registers your brand and campaign with The Campaign Registry. Unregistered traffic is likely to be filtered or blocked by the carriers. Your provider handles the forms, and some charge a small one-time and monthly fee.

Is it legal to text someone who called my business? A single reply about the call they just made is the safest kind of business text, because it responds to them. Keep it about their call, with no promotions. Honor STOP, quit, end, cancel and similar replies, and stop texting that number. Promotional texts need written consent. This is not legal advice, so check with your provider or a lawyer for your situation.

How much does missed call text back cost? It is often included in tools you may already pay for. Quo includes missed call auto-replies from $19 per user per month, and HighLevel includes it from $97 a month plus usage. Texting carries per-message fees of around a cent, plus carrier registration fees. A custom build with AI replies costs a setup fee and a monthly fee.

Is missed call text back better than an AI receptionist? It is cheaper, not better. It catches callers who are happy to text. It does not help callers who want an answer on the phone, emergencies, or businesses with high call volume. Start with text back, measure how many missed callers reply, and move to an AI receptionist when the numbers justify it.

What should a missed call text back message say? Name the business, say sorry for missing the call, and ask one question. For example: Hi, this is Lake Plumbing. Sorry we missed your call. What can we help with? Reply STOP to opt out. Keep it short, send it once per caller in a set window, and never add a promotion.
