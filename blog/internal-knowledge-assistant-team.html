Internal knowledge assistant: stop your best people being the company search engine

By Babar Lal  ·  6 October 2026  ·  9 min read

It is Friday afternoon. A new hire walks over to the office manager's desk. "Sorry, how do I log a warranty job again?"

It is the third time this week. The answer is in a document somewhere, but nobody knows which one. The office manager explains it again, then needs a few minutes to find her place in the payroll she was checking. By the end of the week, that adds up to another hour.

The short answer

Give staff a chat tool that answers from your own documents and shows its source. Your senior people then answer only the questions nobody has written down yet.

How much time goes on searching and asking

The most quoted study is old. McKinsey's article Capturing business value with social technologies was published in November 2012. That makes it 14 years old. I opened the original on McKinsey's own site. It found interaction workers spent 19% of an average workweek searching for and gathering information.

A newer figure comes from a vendor. Atlassian, which sells the Confluence wiki, surveyed 12,000 knowledge workers and 200 executives for its State of Teams 2025 report. It says teams "waste 25% of their time just searching for answers".

Neither study measures what I care about most here. That is the time lost by the person who gets asked. A question that takes two minutes to answer can cost far more in lost focus. I could not find an open study that measures this for small businesses, so the cost sum below uses my own assumption.

What RAG means in plain English

RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation. The name is worse than the idea.

The assistant searches your own documents first. It finds the few passages that match the question. It writes the answer from those passages. And it shows which document it used, so staff can check.

A general chatbot works differently. It answers from what it learned in training. It has never seen your warranty process, your price list or your leave policy. Ask it anyway and it may write something that sounds right and is not. For internal questions, an answer with a source beats a confident guess.

How it works, step by step

  1. Gather the documents. Process guides, price lists, policies, templates, past job notes and help desk answers.
  2. Clean out old versions. If three copies of the price list exist, delete two. The assistant cannot tell which one is current.
  3. Connect the sources. That might be a shared drive, a wiki, a help desk or a folder of PDFs. New and changed files should flow in automatically.
  4. Staff ask where they already work. That might be Slack, Microsoft Teams or a simple web page.
  5. The answer cites its source. Every answer links to the document and section it came from.
  6. Gaps get flagged. When there is no good source, the assistant says so and logs the question. Someone then writes the missing document.

That last step is the one owners forget. The questions the assistant cannot answer are a free list of the documents your business is missing.

This is the same idea as an AI website chat that answers customers from your own information, pointed inward at staff. It is a different job from pulling numbers out of invoices and receipts.

Access, privacy and accuracy

This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Who can see what. The assistant should only search files the person asking could already open. Microsoft's own guidance says Copilot "only surfaces organizational data to which individual users have at least view permissions". That sounds safe. But it also means any folder shared too widely, years ago, becomes easy to find. Microsoft's SharePoint readiness guide spends most of its length on finding overshared sites for exactly this reason.

Keep HR, salary and client-confidential files out at first. Add them only once permissions are enforced and tested with a real staff account.

What the provider does with your data. On business plans, the big providers say they do not train on it by default. Microsoft says prompts, responses and data accessed through Microsoft Graph "aren't used to train foundation LLMs". Google says Workspace content is not "used for Generative AI model training outside your domain without permission". OpenAI says it does not use ChatGPT Business data for training by default. Anthropic says it will not use chats from its commercial products for training, unless the customer opts in. Personal accounts have different terms, so keep staff on the business plan.

Saying "I don't know". A good assistant refuses to answer when it has no source. Test this on purpose. Ask it something your documents do not cover and see what it does.

When it backfires

Outdated documents. The assistant quotes last year's price list with total confidence. It is only as current as your files. Give every key document an owner and a review date.

Permission leaks. Someone asks about bonuses and gets a line from a salary file that was shared with "everyone" in 2019. Audit sharing before you switch it on.

Nobody maintaining the source documents. The assistant works well for three months. Then processes change and nobody updates the guides. Answers drift until staff stop asking.

Staff losing trust after one bad answer. People forgive a colleague who gets it wrong. They rarely forgive a tool. Start with the documents you know are right, and make it easy to report a wrong answer.

Questions people ask

What is an internal AI knowledge assistant? It is a chat tool for your staff that answers questions from your own documents: process guides, price lists, policies and past job notes. It shows which document each answer came from. When it cannot find a source, it says so instead of guessing.

What does RAG mean? RAG stands for retrieval-augmented generation. In plain English, the assistant searches your documents first, then writes its answer from the passages it found, and links to them. A general chatbot answers from what it learned in training, which knows nothing about your business.

Can we just upload our documents to ChatGPT or Claude? For a small team with a handful of documents, yes, that can be a good start. Use a business plan, not a personal account. It gets harder when you have hundreds of files, documents that change every month, or files that only some people may see.

Will the AI provider train its models on our documents? On business plans, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI and Anthropic all say on their own policy pages that they do not train their models on your business data by default. Read the current page for the exact plan you buy, because the terms differ between personal and business plans.

How do we stop staff seeing files they should not? The assistant should only search files the person asking could already open. Fix sharing settings before you switch it on, because loose permissions that nobody noticed become easy to find. Keep salary, HR and client-confidential files out until permissions are tested.

What happens when the assistant gives a wrong answer? Usually the source document is wrong or out of date. Because every answer names its source, staff can check it and report it. Fix the document, not just the answer. Then the next person gets the right answer too.
