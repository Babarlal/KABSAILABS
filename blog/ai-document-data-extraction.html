AI document data extraction: stop typing numbers off invoices, receipts and forms

By Babar Lal  ·  6 October 2026  ·  9 min read

It is Monday morning. There is a stack of supplier invoices on the desk and more in the inbox. Someone opens each one, finds the total and types it into QuickBooks. Then the date, the supplier and the invoice number.

One invoice says $1,480. It goes in as $1,840. Nobody notices. At month end the supplier statement does not match, and someone spends an afternoon finding one swapped digit.

The short answer

Let software read the paperwork, check it against your rules and post it as a draft. A person approves the routine ones in one click and looks closely only at the unsure ones.

What manual data entry costs

Most of the research here comes from companies that sell accounts payable software.

The State of ePayables 2025 report by Ardent Partners is sponsored by Bottomline, a payments company. It found only 51% of invoices arrive electronically. It also says the leading teams process invoices 79% faster and at 79% lower cost than the rest.

APQC, a benchmarking group, publishes a cost per invoice too. Its figures are for members only, so I could not read them and I am not quoting them.

The bigger risk is paying the wrong person. The Association for Financial Professionals' 2025 Payments Fraud and Control Survey, published in April 2025 and sponsored by Truist, asked 521 finance staff about 2024. 79% of their organizations faced attempted or actual payments fraud. 45% saw vendor imposter fraud, where someone poses as a real supplier. Most were larger companies. A small office simply has fewer people checking.

I could not find an open, independent study of typing error rates in small offices. So the error rate in the cost sum below is my assumption.

Old OCR vs AI extraction

Old OCR turned a scan into text. It did not know which number was the total. Invoice tools fixed that with templates, one per supplier, telling the software where each field sat. A new supplier meant a new template. A supplier who moved their logo broke it.

AI extraction reads the page more like a person does. It finds the total because it looks like a total, wherever it sits. Amazon's Textract documentation says its invoice reader works on "almost any invoice or receipt without the need for any templates or configuration". That is a vendor claim. It also returns a confidence score for each field, and that matters more.

A human check still matters because AI is confidently wrong sometimes. It can mix up a unit price and a line total, or read a 7 as a 1 on a creased receipt. The trick is to let the confidence scores and your rules decide which documents a person needs to see.

How it works, step by step

  1. Documents arrive. Suppliers email invoices to one address, staff upload PDFs, or someone photographs a receipt.
  2. AI reads them. It pulls the supplier, date, invoice number, totals, tax and line items, with a confidence score for each.
  3. Rules check them. Does it match the purchase order? Do the lines add up to the total? Have we seen this invoice number from this supplier before?
  4. It posts as a draft. The bill lands in QuickBooks, Xero or your practice system for approval, with the original image attached.
  5. A person approves. Routine ones take one click. Anything unsure, unmatched or over a limit goes to a person with a note saying why.

Accuracy and controls

This is general guidance, not legal or tax advice.

Set a confidence threshold. If any key field reads below it, the document goes to a person. Start strict and loosen it once you trust the results.

Approve before posting. Nothing becomes a payable bill until a person says yes.

Protect against duplicates. Check supplier and invoice number, not just amount and date. A Xero user asked for exactly this in a 2022 Hubdoc feature request.

Keep an audit trail. Every entry should link to the original image, who approved it and when. The IRS says to keep records until the period of limitations runs out, which is three years in most cases.

Clinics and anyone handling personal data need more care. Patient intake forms and insurance claims carry health information. Every tool that reads them must be set up for it. Our post on reducing no-shows covers the HIPAA basics, and our healthcare automation page shows what we build for clinics.

When it backfires

Posting without review. If extracted bills go straight to paid, one misread total becomes one wrong payment. Keep approval in the loop.

Handwritten notes or poor scans. A creased receipt photographed in a van at dusk will read badly. Send low-confidence fields to a person, and ask staff to photograph receipts flat and in good light.

Suppliers who change their layout. AI copes far better than templates did. Still, watch the first few invoices after a supplier rebrands.

Duplicate payments. The same invoice arrives by email and by post. Both get read. Without a check on invoice number, both get paid.

Nobody watching the exception queue. The flagged documents pile up, bills go unpaid, and suppliers call. Give the queue an owner and a daily slot.

Questions people ask

What is AI document data extraction? It is software that reads a document, such as an invoice, receipt, purchase order or form, and pulls out the fields you need. That means the supplier, date, invoice number, totals and line items. It then checks them against your rules and puts them into your accounting or practice system, usually as a draft for a person to approve.

Can QuickBooks read invoices automatically? Partly. QuickBooks Online lets you email or upload receipts and bills. Its help page says it pulls the date, amount, vendor and last four digits of the card from the image. You then review each one in the Receipts tab and add it or match it to a bank transaction.

How accurate is AI invoice extraction? I have not found an independent accuracy figure I trust. Most published numbers come from vendors testing their own tools. Clean PDFs from known suppliers usually read well. Handwriting and poor phone photos do not. Test any tool on your own last 100 documents before you rely on it.

Is OCR the same as AI extraction? No. Plain OCR turns an image into text. Older invoice tools then needed a template for each supplier to know where the total sat. AI extraction reads the layout like a person would, so a new supplier usually works without a template. It still makes mistakes, so it still needs checks.

How do I stop duplicate invoice payments? Check every new invoice against past ones on supplier and invoice number, not just amount and date. Hold anything that looks like a repeat for a person. Pay only from approved bills, never straight from an email. Confirm any change to a supplier's bank details by phone, using a number you already had.

Is it safe to use for patient or customer documents? It can be, if the tools are set up for it. Keep the data with providers that will sign the agreements your rules need, limit who can see it, and keep a log of who opened what. Clinics should check the HIPAA rules for every tool that touches patient forms. This is not legal advice.
