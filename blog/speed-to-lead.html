Speed to lead: why the first reply decides who gets the job

By Babar Lal  ·  5 October 2026  ·  9 min read

It is 7:40pm. A woman fills in the contact form on a roofer's website. She has a leak over the kitchen. Nothing comes back. At 8:05pm she tries a second roofer. That one texts her within a minute and books a visit for Thursday. The first company sees her enquiry at 8:30 the next morning. She has already said yes to someone else.

That gap between the form and the first reply is called speed to lead. It is one of the few things in sales you fully control.

The short answer

Fast means minutes, not hours. The first useful reply often wins.

What the research says about lead response time

The two studies everyone quotes are old, so I give their age.

The first is the Harvard Business Review article The Short Life of Online Sales Leads, published in March 2011. That makes it 15 years old. The authors audited 2,241 US companies. 23% never replied to a web lead at all. Firms that made contact within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited even an hour longer. The article is paywalled, so I read the figures in a reprint.

The second is the Lead Response Management study from October 2007, by James Oldroyd of MIT and Dave Elkington, the CEO of InsideSales. That is 19 years old, and one author ran a sales software company. It found the odds of qualifying a lead dropped 21 times when the call came at 30 minutes instead of 5.

Treat both as dated. The direction is clear. I would not plan a 2026 business around the exact multipliers.

Newer data points the same way. In a 2024 secret shopper test reported by HousingWire, consultant Mike DelPrete sent 100 inquiries to more than 25 US brokerages. 47% got no reply. The median reply took 39 minutes. A 2025 study by DAS Technology, a vendor, checked 1,700 car dealership web leads. 61% got a reply within 15 minutes, and 19% waited more than an hour.

I could not find a recent, openable study for home services or clinic web forms. If you know of one, I would like to read it.

Why leads go cold

After-hours enquiries. People fill in forms after dinner and at weekends. If your team works nine to five, those leads wait until morning.

Leads sitting where nobody looks. Facebook lead ads keep leads inside the ad account unless you connect them to something. Portal leads land in one agent's email. Website forms go to an info@ inbox everyone assumes someone else checks.

Round-robin delays. Routing a lead to the next agent in line sounds fair. If that agent is at a showing, the lead waits until they are free. Our post on sorting and routing enquiries with AI covers routing by fit, with a time limit before a lead moves on.

No follow-up after the first reply. A lead who did not answer the first message is not the same as a lead who said no.

How an instant reply works, step by step

  1. A lead comes in from a web form, an ad lead form, a portal like Zillow, or a website chat.
  2. Within seconds, the system sends a short text and an email.
  3. The text asks one or two qualifying questions. For a roof, that might be whether water is coming in now.
  4. Based on the answers, it sends a booking link, or it alerts a person to call now. Our post on automatic appointment booking covers how to offer real open slots.
  5. Every message, answer and booking is logged in your CRM against that contact, so nobody starts from scratch.

The instant part is easy. The hard part is step four: someone picking up hot leads within minutes.

The rules for texting web leads in the US

This is a plain summary, not legal advice. Check your setup with your provider or a lawyer.

Consent first. The FCC rule at 47 CFR 64.1200 requires prior express written consent for marketing texts sent with automated systems. That means a signed written agreement, and an electronic signature counts. It must say clearly that agreeing is not a condition of buying. In practice, add a consent line to your form that names your business and is not pre-ticked. Ad and portal leads come with the platform's own consent wording. Read it before you text, because it may not cover marketing texts from you.

Keep the first reply about their enquiry. The CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices separate conversational, informational and promotional texts. Promotional texts need express written consent. A reply to a question someone just asked sits at the safer end.

Honor STOP. Under the same FCC rule, replies like stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel and unsubscribe withdraw consent. You may send one text confirming the opt-out, with no marketing in it. Then the system must stop texting that number.

Your number also needs carrier registration. I covered A2P 10DLC in our post on missed call text back and the US texting rules.

When instant replies backfire

A robotic message can do harm. "Thank you for your inquiry, a team member will be in touch" tells the lead nothing.

Wrong answers are worse than slow ones. An AI that guesses a price, or says a house is available when it sold last week, loses trust fast. It should answer only from your information and hand off when unsure.

The worst case is a reply nobody follows up. The lead answers your questions and then hears nothing. The fix is an alert to a real person and a follow-up sequence for leads who go quiet.

Questions people ask

What is speed to lead? Speed to lead is the time between a new enquiry arriving and your first real reply. The shorter that gap, the more likely you reach the person before a competitor does.

How fast should you respond to a lead? I would aim for an automatic first reply in seconds, then a person or a useful answer within five minutes in working hours. The older research found the odds of qualifying a lead fall sharply within the first hour.

What is a good lead response time in real estate? Under five minutes for the first reply, day or night. In a 2024 secret shopper test by Mike DelPrete, 47% of online property inquiries got no reply at all, and the median reply took 39 minutes.

Can I text a lead who filled in my web form? A reply about the enquiry they just made is the safer kind of text. Marketing texts need prior express written consent under FCC rules, so add a clear consent line to your form. Honor STOP replies straight away. This is not legal advice, so check with your provider or a lawyer.

Will an instant reply feel robotic? It will if it reads like a template and nobody follows it up. Use what they asked about, ask one question, and have a real person pick up the thread within minutes. Never pretend an automated message came from a named agent.

What does an instant lead response system cost? Built-in CRM auto-responders cost from about $20 to $97 a month, depending on the tool. Zapier and Make start at about $12 to $20 a month, plus texting fees. A custom build with AI replies has a setup fee and a monthly fee.
