A post-purchase sequence in GoHighLevel that runs a subscriber's first ninety days

When someone subscribes, it tags them, adds them to the pipeline, welcomes them, shows them how to use the product, sets honest expectations, checks in at 30, 60 and 90 days, then asks for a review.

The problem

Subscription businesses rarely lose customers because the product is bad. They lose them in the first ninety days. A new subscriber is not sure how to use it, sees no instant result, loses faith and cancels.

The fix is known: onboard properly, be honest about how long results take, and keep showing up. Doing that by hand for every customer stops working after the first dozen.

How it works

The workflow starts when a customer is marked as paid. In this build that is a tag. In a live account it hangs off the Stripe payment trigger, with nothing else changing.

  1. Tag the contact active_subscriber.
  2. Create an opportunity in the Subscribers pipeline at Active.
  3. Send a welcome email.
  4. Wait one day, then send an onboarding email on how to use the product.
  5. Wait three days, then send the expectation setting email.
  6. At day 30, send a check-in with a prompt to take a progress photo.
  7. At day 60, the same again.
  8. At day 90, ask for a review and a referral.

The expectation setting email does the most work. It says plainly that results take months, that early ups and downs are normal, and that the people who get the best results are the ones who stay consistent. It goes out before doubt sets in.

What a person still handles

  • The copy. For regulated businesses such as health or supplements, the brand supplies its own approved text and it drops into the same steps.
  • Replies to the check-ins.
  • Reviews and referrals once they come in.

Built with

GoHighLevel workflows, tags, pipelines and opportunities, and the email builder. It is one part of a connected lifecycle, alongside failed payment recovery, refill reminders and cancellation win-back, linked through shared tags and pipeline stages.
