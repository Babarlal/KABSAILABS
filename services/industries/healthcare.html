◔ A day on the phones

The calls land when the desk is busiest.

Opening rush, lunch, and the closing wind-down account for most missed calls in business hours, and the biggest after-hours wave is 5–9 PM when patients finally have time. The front desk isn't slacking; it's physically with someone.

Calls answered by the desk. Mostly mid-morning and early afternoon, when nobody's checking in.

✦ Clinical safety

It books appointments. It does not give medical advice.

The agent handles scheduling, intake and reminders. The moment a caller describes symptoms, pain, an emergency or asks a clinical question, it stops and hands to your team, with the call transcript attached.

  • Clinical questions routed to a person, never answered by the agent
  • Emergencies rung straight through, not queued
  • Call recordings and transcripts available to the practice
  • Identifies itself as an assistant if you want it to
  • Runs on your number, or a new after-hours line

FAQ

What practice managers ask first.

Will patients be upset they got an AI?+
Most just want to be answered and booked. It sounds natural, picks up on the first ring, and can say it's an assistant. The alternative they're comparing it to is voicemail, which 78% of them hang up on.
What happens with an emergency or a clinical question?+
It stops and hands to a person, immediately, with the transcript. It never answers a clinical question itself. Emergencies ring through rather than wait.
Does it work with our practice management software?+
It books into the calendar you run and logs the call. We'll confirm your specific PMS on the call; most with a calendar API or a booking link are straightforward.
How is it priced?+
A fixed build fee, then a monthly fee to keep it running. Never per call, so a busy month doesn't cost more.
